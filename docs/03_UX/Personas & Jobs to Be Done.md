# 10. Users & Jobs to Be Done

## Overview

Healthcare users do not visit PocketPills because they want to use a healthcare platform.

They visit because they have a problem they need solved.

Understanding these motivations is more valuable than understanding demographics alone.

This chapter identifies the primary user groups, their goals, frustrations, motivations, and the jobs they are trying to accomplish.

These insights will guide every product and design decision throughout the redesign.

---

# Primary User Segments

## Busy Professionals

### Profile

Working adults with limited time who value convenience and efficiency.

### Goals

- Get treatment quickly
- Avoid waiting rooms
- Refill prescriptions online
- Receive medication at home

### Pain Points

- Long clinic wait times
- Limited pharmacy hours
- Managing healthcare around work

### Success Looks Like

Healthcare fits into their schedule instead of disrupting it.

---

## Families & Caregivers

### Profile

Parents or caregivers responsible for managing healthcare for multiple people.

### Goals

- Manage medications for family members
- Coordinate appointments
- Access care quickly
- Keep health information organized

### Pain Points

- Multiple providers
- Separate health records
- Remembering medications
- Scheduling appointments

### Success Looks Like

One place to manage the entire family's healthcare.

---

## Chronic Care Patients

### Profile

Patients managing long-term conditions requiring ongoing medication and regular follow-up.

### Goals

- Never miss medication
- Easily refill prescriptions
- Monitor treatment progress
- Stay connected with healthcare providers

### Pain Points

- Frequent refills
- Medication adherence
- Fragmented healthcare experiences

### Success Looks Like

Healthcare becomes predictable and easy to manage.

---

## Seniors

### Profile

Older adults who prioritize trust, simplicity, and accessibility.

### Goals

- Easy medication management
- Reliable delivery
- Clear instructions
- Family support

### Pain Points

- Complex healthcare systems
- Transportation
- Multiple prescriptions

### Success Looks Like

Healthcare feels simple and dependable.

---

# Jobs to Be Done

## Job 01

### When I need medical advice...

I want to quickly understand what care I need,

so that I can take the right next step without confusion.

---

## Job 02

### When I need medication...

I want to receive it without unnecessary trips or delays,

so that managing my health feels effortless.

---

## Job 03

### When my prescription expires...

I want to renew it easily,

so that my treatment continues without interruption.

---

## Job 04

### When I'm managing a chronic condition...

I want everything related to my treatment in one place,

so that I don't have to coordinate multiple systems.

---

## Job 05

### When caring for my family...

I want to organize everyone's healthcare together,

so that nothing gets forgotten.

---

## Job 06

### When I'm unsure about my symptoms...

I want guidance,

so that I know whether I need treatment, a consultation, or reassurance.

---

# Common User Goals

Across all user groups, the goals remain consistent.

Users want to:

- Find the right care quickly
- Avoid unnecessary effort
- Understand what happens next
- Trust the process
- Stay healthy with minimal stress

---

# Common Pain Points

Research identified recurring frustrations across the healthcare journey.

### Before Care

- Don't know where to start
- Unsure which service they need
- Long wait times
- Complex healthcare systems

---

### During Care

- Multiple steps
- Repeating information
- Lack of transparency
- Limited guidance

---

### After Care

- Forgetting medication
- Missing follow-ups
- Poor continuity
- No ongoing support

---

# User Motivations

People choose digital healthcare because they value:

- Convenience
- Accessibility
- Speed
- Trust
- Privacy
- Simplicity
- Confidence

The redesign should reinforce these motivations at every stage.

---

# User Expectations

Today's healthcare users expect experiences comparable to modern consumer apps.

They expect:

- Clear onboarding
- Personalized recommendations
- Real-time updates
- Transparent timelines
- Mobile-first experiences
- Intelligent guidance
- Continuous support

---

# Behavioral Insights

Healthcare users think in outcomes.

Not services.

Examples

❌ I need a consultation.

✔ I have a rash.

❌ I need a pharmacy.

✔ I ran out of medication.

❌ I need prescription management.

✔ I don't want to miss my medicine.

The experience should begin with user intentions rather than internal healthcare terminology.

---

# Design Implications

Based on user research, the redesign should:

- Start with user intent.
- Reduce cognitive load.
- Guide users through decisions.
- Personalize healthcare journeys.
- Support users beyond treatment.
- Create one connected healthcare experience.

# User Needs Summary

| User Need | Product Response |
|------------|------------------|
| I need help quickly | Intent-based homepage |
| I don't know what to do | Guided care pathways |
| I want fewer steps | Simplified onboarding |
| I need transparency | Clear timelines & pricing |
| I want everything together | Unified health dashboard |
| I don't want to forget | Smart reminders & follow-ups |
| I want ongoing support | Continuous care experience |

---

# Key Insight

The redesign is not about making healthcare faster.

It is about making healthcare feel easier.

Users are not looking for more healthcare services.

They are looking for a healthcare experience that understands them, guides them, and stays with them over time.

This insight becomes the foundation for the information architecture, user flows, and interaction design in the following chapters.
